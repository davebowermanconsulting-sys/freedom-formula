# Malang Indigenous Corporation: platform report
## E-commerce and online training platforms for 2027

- **Prepared by:** Freedom Founders
- **Date:** 16 November 2026
- **Status:** DRAFT for discussion with Saretta and the Malang team

---

## 1. Executive summary

From the new year, Malang wants one online home that lets it:

1. **Host and sell online training.** That means cultural awareness, art workshops and RAP-related learning, bought by individuals and by organisations buying seats for staff.
2. **Sell art made by community members of the Artists Together program,** with artists paid fairly and transparently.
3. **Keep telling Malang's story:** programs, projects, events, consulting, raffles and donations.

**Recommendation in one line:** stay on **Wix** for 2027 and grow into the new functions in phases. Sell art as a **Malang-curated consignment shop** (Malang is the seller of record and pays artists), not an open multi-vendor marketplace. Pilot courses on **Wix Online Programs**, and add a dedicated course platform (**LearnWorlds** or **Thinkific**) only if corporate training demand outgrows it. Re-assess **Shopify** at the 12-month mark if art sales volume justifies a move.

Why:
- **No rebuild.** Malang's whole site, brand, blog, forms and raffle already live on Wix, and we are finishing the refresh now. A platform migration in early 2027 would spend the budget on re-doing work instead of launching the new offers.
- **Lowest running cost and lowest admin load** for a not-for-profit team: one login, one bill, one checkout.
- **The consignment model fits Malang's purpose and obligations.** Malang stays accountable to artists under the Indigenous Art Code, artists don't have to run their own seller accounts, and a single checkout serves buyers.
- **It keeps the door open.** Every step is reversible, and nothing locks Malang out of moving to Shopify or a dedicated course platform later.

## 3. The key decision before choosing a platform: how art is sold

There are two ways to "let artists sell on the Malang website". The choice affects platform, cost, legal set-up and workload far more than which website builder Malang uses.

| | **A. Curated consignment shop (recommended)** | **B. Open multi-vendor marketplace** |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | Malang lists selected works on artists' behalf. Buyers pay Malang. Malang pays the artist their share and issues a written statement. | Each artist gets a seller account, uploads their own products and is paid out automatically, minus a commission. |
| Who is the seller to the buyer | Malang | Each artist (with Malang as the platform) |
| Artist workload | Low. Malang photographs, lists, ships and handles customers. | High. Artists manage listings, stock, shipping and customer questions. |
| Indigenous Art Code fit | Strong. Matches the Code's model of agreements, records and regular written reports to artists. | Harder. Malang must still police fair dealing across many sellers. |
| Platform need | Any standard online store | Needs a third-party marketplace app on Wix or Shopify, or a specialist marketplace platform |
| Cost and complexity | Low | Medium–high (app fees, vendor onboarding, payout set-up, support) |
| Best when | Up to roughly a few dozen artists and a curated range | Many independent sellers wanting self-service |

**Recommendation: start with A.** Malang's role is a facilitator and supporter of artists. The consignment model lets Malang curate quality, protect artists and keep one simple checkout. If, after 12 months, artists ask to manage their own stores, that is the trigger to look at B.

What A needs in practice (whatever the platform):
- A **written consignment agreement** with each artist covering commission split, pricing, payment timing, insurance, returns, and licensing of images for promotion.
- An **artist field on every product** (for example a "Artist" collection/tag), so sales reports can be run per artist.
- **Monthly artist statements** listing artwork received, sold, sale price, artist payment and Malang commission. These are the items the Indigenous Art Code expects dealers to report.
- **Dealer membership of the Indigenous Art Code** (listed at $168 per year on the Code's website at the time of writing; confirm before applying). The Code's badge is also a strong trust signal for buyers.
- **Resale royalty awareness.** Under Australia's Resale Royalty scheme, artists receive 5% on eligible *resales* of original artworks (not first sales) of $1,000 or more by art-market professionals. If Malang ever resells works (rather than selling first-hand on consignment), confirm the obligations with Copyright Agency.

## 4. Platforms assessed

We assessed three website platforms (Wix, Shopify, Squarespace) and four dedicated course platforms that could sit beside the website (Thinkific, Teachable, LearnWorlds, Kajabi).

**Pricing note:** prices were checked on 16 November 2026 from vendor pricing pages where reachable, and otherwise from 2026 third-party reviews (marked †). Vendors change prices and run promotions often, so confirm on the vendor's page before committing. Card-processing fees are additional to plan fees unless stated.

### 4.1 Wix (current platform)

- **Plans (annual billing, per month):** Light ~$17, Core ~$29, Business ~$39, Business Elite ~$159. The Wix pricing page showed "Price includes GST"; confirm the AUD amount in the Malang account before upgrading. To accept online payments you need Core or above.
- **Courses:** **Wix Online Programs** (native app) gives step-based programs with video, quizzes, certificates of completion, groups and unlimited participants. It sells programs one-off or through **Wix Pricing Plans** (memberships). [Verify which plan tier unlocks paid programs before purchase.]
- **Art sales:** **Wix Stores** (native) handles one-off originals (stock quantity 1), prints and product variants. Multi-vendor is **not native**; it is available only via third-party App Market apps (for example "Multi Vendor Store", Webkul "Multi Vendor Marketplace") of mixed quality and ratings.
- **Corporate seat sales:** limited natively. Possible through coupons/manual enrolment; true team-seat management is weak.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Already Malang's platform. No migration, refresh work is reused | Online Programs is simpler than a dedicated LMS (no SCORM, limited team/seat admin, basic reporting) |
| One login, one bill, one checkout for courses + art + donations + raffle | Multi-vendor only via third-party apps |
| Strong visual editor for a story-led, art-led brand | Classic Editor sites are ageing; future Wix features land on newer editors first |
| Low cost: Business plan covers store + programs | Store/checkout less powerful than Shopify for high volume |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---|
| Launch courses + art shop quickly in Q1 2027 on existing site | If corporate demand grows fast, Online Programs may need replacing |
| Bundles (for example workshop + artwork, course + print) in one checkout | Reliance on third-party apps if multi-vendor is ever wanted |
| Pricing Plans for memberships / supporter tiers | Wix price rises (Wix raised prices in 2025) |

### 4.2 Shopify

- **Plans (AUD, verified on shopify.com/au/pricing, 16 Nov 2026):** Basic **$42/mo** yearly ($56 monthly), Grow **$114/mo** yearly ($149 monthly), Advanced **$431/mo** yearly ($575 monthly), Plus from $3,700/mo. Online card rates with Shopify Payments from about **1.7% + 30¢** (Basic) and lower on higher plans. A surcharge applies if a third-party payment provider is used. Current promotion: 3-day trial, then $1/month for 3 months.
- **Courses:** **not native.** Needs an app (for example Tevello, Courses Plus, Elite Courses, ShoppyLMS) or a linked LMS such as Thinkific. App fees are extra.
- **Art sales:** best-in-class store and checkout. Multi-vendor via mature apps (for example Puppet Vendors from about US$49/mo†, MarketFox from about US$29/mo†, plus payout tools such as CollabPay).
- **Our team's experience:** we run Saretta Art & Design on Shopify, so we know the platform's strengths at scale first-hand.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Strongest e-commerce engine, checkout and payments in Australia | Full migration and rebuild of the Malang site required |
| Mature multi-vendor and vendor-payout apps | Courses depend on third-party apps, so course UX and data are split |
| Large app ecosystem, strong reporting | Content/story pages less flexible than Wix |
| Scales to high order volumes | Monthly cost climbs quickly (plan + several paid apps) |

| Opportunities | Threats |
|---|---|
| If art + products become a major revenue stream, Shopify is the natural home | Rebuild cost and time delay the 2027 launch |
| Wholesale/B2B and licensing products later | App sprawl increases admin for a small team |
| Shop channels (Instagram, Google) | NFP features (donations, raffle) need extra apps |

### 4.3 Squarespace

- **Plans (USD, from squarespace.com/pricing, 16 Nov 2026):** Basic $19/mo annual ($25 monthly), Core $29 ($39 monthly)†, Plus $49 ($65 monthly), Advanced $99 ($139 monthly). Online store transaction fees: 2% on Basic, 0% on higher plans. Card rates from 2.9% + 30¢ down to 2.5% + 30¢.
- **Courses:** native **Courses** and member areas. Clean and simple. But **digital-product fees** apply by tier (reported as 7% Basic, 5% Core, 1% Plus, 0% Advanced†).
- **Art sales:** good for a single seller. **No multi-vendor** capability.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Beautiful templates, simple to run | Full migration required |
| Native courses and memberships | No multi-vendor; weaker store than Shopify |
| | Fees on course sales unless on top tier |
| | Fewer apps/integrations than Wix or Shopify |

**Verdict:** Squarespace doesn't beat Wix on any requirement that matters to Malang, and it would cost a migration. **Not recommended.**

| Platform | Indicative price (USD/mo) | Good at | Watch-outs |

## 6. Competitor and market landscape

Malang would operate in two overlapping markets: **ethical Aboriginal art sales online** and **Aboriginal cultural learning / awareness training**. Very few organisations combine both, and fewer still are **Aboriginal-owned and controlled not-for-profits with a living artist program**. That combination is Malang's edge.

### 6.1 Online Aboriginal art sellers

| Organisation | Model | Ownership (as described publicly) | What Malang can learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bluethumb | Large national online art marketplace; works with remote Aboriginal art centres | Not Aboriginal-owned | Buyers expect great photography, sizes, shipping clarity and artist profiles |
| Yarn Marketplace | Indigenous-design products and art | Reported as non-Indigenous owned | Strong product range and storytelling, but authenticity questions are a market risk Malang can answer |
| Blackspace Creative (Tas) | Online store of art and cultural works by Tasmanian artists | Aboriginal-owned | Regional, place-based curation works |
| Tarkiner Treasures (Tas) | Online marketplace run by a Tasmanian Aboriginal corporation | Aboriginal corporation | Closest model to Malang: a corporation running a marketplace for community artisans |
| Yamaji Art (WA) | Art centre with online store | Reported as 100% Indigenous-owned | Art-centre model: artist representation plus online sales |
| Aboriginal Art Co (Qld) | Gallery, products and online store | Described as Aboriginal-owned and operated | Mix of originals and homewares widens price points |

Ownership claims come from public marketing or directory pages and haven't been independently verified.

### 6.2 Online cultural awareness / cultural learning providers

| Provider | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Evolve Communities | Online and in-person cultural awareness training; "Ally Accreditation" | Well-known corporate provider; ownership not confirmed |
| Marumali Program | Cultural awareness program online | Established program |
| AIATSIS / ANU "Core Cultural Learning" | Online foundation course | Widely used by government and universities; low-cost benchmark |
| SBS × Carbon Creative course | Online course created by a First Nations creative agency | Shows demand for high-production, story-led learning |
| VACCA (Vic) | Cultural awareness training for individuals and organisations | Aboriginal community-controlled organisation model |
| Walya Productions | National e-learning course | Ownership not confirmed |

**Where Malang fits:** most providers sell *generic* national training. Malang can offer **local, artist-led, art-based** learning connected to real artists, artworks and projects in the Lake Macquarie / Hunter region and beyond. That is a hands-on, story-rich alternative to slide-deck compliance training, and it naturally leads buyers into commissions, workshops and art purchases.

## 7. SWOT: Malang as course host, facilitator and art-sales operator

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|
| Aboriginal-owned and controlled not-for-profit with cultural authority | Small team; limited staff time to run a shop and courses |
| Founder and CEO Saretta Fielding is an established international artist with a proven art business | No existing course content. It must be scripted, filmed and edited |
| Artists Together program: engagement with over 100 artists since 2016 (per Malang website) | No e-commerce operations yet (photography, listing, shipping, artist statements) |
| Existing corporate and government relationships (for example Winc Australia, Arcadis, GrainCorp, WESNET, as shown on the website) | Website mid-refresh; mobile layout issues on some pages |
| Registered with the ACNC; trust and grant eligibility | Brand awareness concentrated locally |
| Sister experience: SAAD's proven e-commerce growth | |

| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
|---|---|
| Ongoing demand from organisations for cultural learning linked to Reconciliation Action Plans | Inauthentic "Aboriginal-style" products undercut prices and confuse buyers |
| Bundle training + workshops + artwork commissions for corporate clients | Established, cheaper national e-learning providers |
| Recurring revenue via memberships or annual corporate licences | Cultural/IP risk if content or artwork is shared without proper permissions |
| Indigenous Art Code membership as a trust badge | Platform price rises and app fees eroding margins |
| Grants and partnerships for digital capability and artist development | Staff burnout if operations scale faster than capacity |
| Brand hero video (planned) to lift conversion across site, socials and grant applications | Payment and fraud risk on high-value original art sales |

### SWOT actions
- **Use strengths against threats:** lead with authenticity. Display Art Code membership, artist bios and stories on every product, and certificates of authenticity.
- **Fix weaknesses to unlock opportunities:** build one flagship course first (see roadmap) and a 10–20 piece curated art launch, not everything at once.
- **Protect against IP risk:** written artist agreements covering ICIP, image licensing and course-content consent before anything goes online.

## 8. Recommended roadmap

| Phase | Timing | What | Platform |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Finish & stabilise | Nov–Dec 2026 | Complete the two outstanding pages, full desktop/mobile touch-up, brand hero video | Wix (current) |
| 1. Foundations | Jan–Feb 2027 | Upgrade to Wix Business plan; artist consignment agreements; Indigenous Art Code dealer membership; product photography; payment set-up | Wix |
| 2. Launch | Mar–Apr 2027 | **Art shop** with 10–20 curated works; **one pilot course** (for example an introductory, artist-led cultural learning course) on Wix Online Programs | Wix |
| 3. Corporate | May–Aug 2027 | Corporate packages (seats + live workshop); measure demand for SCORM/team seats | Wix, adding LearnWorlds/Thinkific if triggered |
| 4. Review | ~Dec 2027 | Review art sales volume and artist demand for self-service | Stay on Wix, or plan a Shopify move with multi-vendor |

**Triggers to change platform:**
- Add a **dedicated course platform** if 2+ corporate clients need SCORM, team dashboards or detailed completion reporting.
- Move to **Shopify** if art/product orders consistently exceed what the Wix store handles comfortably (for example sustained high monthly order volumes, wholesale or many product variants), or if artists want their own seller accounts.
